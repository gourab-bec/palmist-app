# Field & Design-Canon Study

## The race

Six qualified candidates, two seats, four-year terms, nonpartisan, November 3 2026.
Incumbents Ronna Green and Bernice King Tingle; challengers Happy Grewal, Arjun
Juturu, Nader Shareghi, Suresh Vuyyuru. (Seed-sourced; clerk log unreachable — see PHASE0.md.)

**Our posture toward the other five: nothing.** No comparison tables, no scorecards,
no "unlike my opponent," no keyword pages targeting their names. `/voter-info` lists
all six alphabetically with the clerk citation and no adjectives, and that is the
entire extent of it. This is a §3.4 rule, but it is also strategy: in a city of
~30,000 where candidates share school pickup lines and Rotary breakfasts, the
attacking candidate loses. The site's argument is a receipt, not a takedown.

**Structural read of the race.** Two seats and six names means roughly 25–30% of the
vote likely wins one. Turnout in a first-cycle municipal general is unpredictable.
The persuadable universe is small and reachable, which is why the site is optimized
for *depth on arrival* (proof, receipts, volunteer conversion) rather than reach.
A resident who arrives already curious is the whole target.

## Design canon — what we took, what we refused

**Pete for America (Hyperakt).** Took: the public toolkit. The insight that made
that campaign visually enormous was that supporters were handed a real design kit
and made the internet look like one campaign for free. Our `/toolkit` is that move
at city scale — yard sign, profile frame, sticker, story cards, all SVG + print PDF,
with the FPPC disclaimer burned into every generated share image so a volunteer
*cannot* accidentally publish an illegal graphic. Refused: the palette, the logo,
the copy voice.

**Warren 2020.** Took: policy as information architecture — issues that are readable
documents with a clear top-line, not a wall of pledges. Adapted down hard: Suresh
has publicly named issues, not published white papers. Writing him a 40-page platform
would be inventing facts (§4). So `/issues` is six short stories in his register,
each one anchored to something he has actually said or done.

**Obama 2008/2012.** Took: volunteer conversion as the primary CTA, and "this is
bigger than a bio." Translated to ~30k people: the movement is already real and it
is not the campaign — it is the volleyball tournament, the cultural night, the blood
drive. The site's job is to show a movement that predates the candidacy by six years.

**AOC 2018.** Took only the method: refuse the default party palette and own a color
that belongs to a place. Delta Gold Civic is harvest gold and civic teal on paper.
Took nothing partisan — this is a nonpartisan race and §3.4 governs.

**USWDS + GOV.UK.** Took: accessibility patterns that are already battle-tested
(focus states, error summaries, skip links, plain language). Refused: the visual
flatness. Government-trust patterns wearing neighbor-warm clothes.

**Apple marketing pages.** Took: scroll choreography discipline — motion that
explains, at 8px not 80px. Refused: the coldness and the video weight.

**MHSRC.org.** Took: the photographic language and the real faces of Mountain House.
Refused: the UI.

## Why a template can't reach this

Campaign Partner, PoliEngine, NationBuilder, Squarespace and the rest solve
"a candidate needs a website by Friday." They are correct for that problem. They
cannot produce a Neighborhood Atlas, service receipts wired to a citation graph, a
retrieval-only bot with a published eval report, or a colophon — because those are
not template slots, they are arguments. The gap we are exploiting is that every
other site in this race will be a template, and a resident can feel the difference
in three seconds without knowing why.
